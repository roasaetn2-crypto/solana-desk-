# TP1 / TP2: measured, and the answer is uncomfortable

Question: should Groq recommend TP1 and TP2 levels?

Answer: **it does not matter, because the rungs are unreachable.** Measured on 2,460
independent setups across the 12 cached pools, using the desk's own entry signal.

## How far price actually runs after an entry

Maximum favourable excursion (MFE), percent above entry, within N bars:

| Horizon | p25 | p50 | p75 | p90 | mean | % that never go green |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 12 bars | 0.27% | 0.62% | 1.94% | 6.86% | 2.96% | 3.1% |
| 24 bars | 0.34% | **0.84%** | 2.65% | 10.43% | 4.10% | 2.1% |
| 48 bars | 0.44% | 1.10% | 3.79% | 13.98% | 5.71% | 1.4% |

The median trade goes **+0.84%** in its favour at best, over 24 bars.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.01x | 45.4% | 33.6% | +0.65% |

| 1.03x | 23.3% | 18.2% | +2.65% |
| **1.05x** (settings minimum) | **17.8%** | **15.0%** | **+4.65%** |
| 1.08x | 13.0% | 11.6% | +7.65% |
| 1.10x | 10.6% | 9.6% | +9.65% |
| 1.15x | 5.9% | 5.2% | +14.65% |
| 1.20x | 4.0% | 3.6% | +19.65% |
| **1.30x** (`scalper` preset) | **2.2%** | **2.0%** | +29.65% |

| 1.80x | 0.4% | 0.4% | +79.65% |

**The `scalper` preset's TP1 at 1.30x fires on 2.2% of setups.** TP2 at 1.80x fires on
0.4%. In practice the ladder is decoration: 97.8% of trades exit through the stop or
the trail and never touch a take-profit rung at all.

Even the tightest value `settings.py` will accept — `tp1_at` has a floor of 1.05 —
fires on only 17.8% of setups.

## The structural problem underneath

| | |
|---|---:|
| Median stop distance (2.5 x ATR) | **1.62%** |
| Median favourable excursion | **0.84%** |
| Median adverse excursion | −1.09% |
| Round-trip cost (fees + slippage) | 0.35% |

You are risking **1.62%** to capture **0.84%**. That is a reward-to-risk ratio of
**0.52 : 1**. To break even you would need a win rate of

```
1 / (1 + 0.52) = 66%
```

The measured win rate is **27.9%**.

This is not a tuning problem, and no take-profit placement fixes it. The stop is
roughly twice the size of the move the strategy is trying to catch. Every trade
starts life needing a 66% hit rate from a signal that delivers 28%.

That single ratio explains every number in this repo: the −23.25% median on unbiased
pools, the −8.75% even with the guard layer, the BIG/TTWO case that ran +1,017%
buy-and-hold while the strategy lost 32.7%.

## So: should Groq pick TP1 and TP2?

**No, and here is the honest reason.**

1. **The rungs are unreachable.** Groq choosing between 1.30x and 1.25x is choosing
   between a 2.2% and a 2.6% chance of ever firing. It is optimising a parameter that
   almost never gets used.

2. **A formula beats a model here.** The right TP is a function of the coin's
   volatility, which you already compute — ATR. `tp1 = entry + 0.5 * ATR` adapts to
   every coin automatically, costs nothing, and **can be backtested**. Groq cannot be
   backtested. Between a testable formula and an untestable opinion on a numeric
   parameter, take the formula.

3. **The real decision is not the TP, it is whether to enter.** Given a 0.52:1
   reward-to-risk, most of these setups should not be taken at all. That is a
   judgement call, and it is exactly where an LLM is genuinely useful — the veto
   layer, ~20 calls/day, not the rung placement.

## What I would actually change

1. **Widen the stop or narrow the target.** Either stop at 1.5 x ATR (median ~0.97%)
   to get reward-to-risk near 1:1, or accept that this signal needs a 66% win rate and
   stop trading it. Right now it has neither.

2. **Drop TP1 to the 1.05–1.10x band** if you keep the ladder at all, and raise
   `tp1_frac` so the rung actually banks something. At 1.05x with a 0.5 fraction you
   bank a small but real amount, and it fires five times more often than 1.30x.

3. **Let Groq veto entries, not set rungs.** One call per candidate setup, ~20/day,
   5% of budget. It sees the 25-field summary it already receives and answers one
   question: is this R:R worth taking. Journaled.

4. **Do not spend another hour tuning TP levels.** The measurement above says the
   parameter is nearly inert. The stop distance is the number that matters.
